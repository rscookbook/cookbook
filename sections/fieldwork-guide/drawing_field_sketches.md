# Field Sketches and Annotated Diagrams
## What Is a Field Sketch?
Field sketches are a valuable way of recording what you observe during fieldwork. They allow you to capture spatial relationships, key features and processes in a way that photographs or written notes alone cannot. Field sketches are especially useful when studying landscapes, river features, coastlines, urban environments or land-use patterns.
A field sketch is a simplified, hand-drawn diagram of a scene or feature observed in the field. It focuses on the most important elements rather than artistic detail. The purpose is accuracy and clarity, not realism.

Field sketches are often annotated to explain:
- What features are present
- Why they are there
- How processes may be shaping them

## What to Include in a Good Field Sketch
A high-quality field sketch should include:
- Clear outlines of major features (e.g. river channel, banks, buildings, vegetation zones)
- Proportions and relative positions that reflect reality (even if not perfectly to scale)
- Labels identifying key features
- Annotations explaining processes or patterns (e.g. erosion, deposition, human activity)
- A title, date, and location
- A direction of view (e.g. “View looking downstream” or a north arrow where appropriate)
Avoid unnecessary detail such as shading, colouring or small background features that do not help answer the enquiry question.

## Annotations: Adding Geographical Understanding
Annotations are short written notes linked to parts of the sketch using lines or arrows. They should go beyond simple labels and show geographical understanding.
Good annotations might:
- Explain processes (e.g. “Faster flow here causes lateral erosion”)
- Link to the enquiry question
- Describe human–environment interactions
- Identify patterns or contrasts

**Weak** annotation: “Trees” vs. **Stronger** annotation: “Dense tree cover stabilises the river bank and reduces erosion”

## Types of Field Sketches
Different types of sketches can be used depending on the investigation:
- Panoramic sketches:
  Wide views showing an entire landscape or urban area, useful for identifying spatial patterns.
- Annotated feature sketches:
  Focused drawings of a single feature such as a meander, beach profile or street junction.
- Process sketches:
  Diagrams that show how a feature is formed or how it changes over time (e.g. erosion and deposition in a river bend).

## How to Draw a Field Sketch Effectively
1. Start by lightly outlining the largest and most important features
2. Add secondary features that help explain the landscape
3. Label clearly using straight lines and neat handwriting
4. Add annotations explaining what is happening and why
5. Check that the sketch links directly to your enquiry question
Field sketches should be completed in the field, not recreated later from memory or photographs.

Happy sketching!
